# Metaworld — Free-to-Play Economy, Media Businesses & No-Safe-Zone World

## Canonical Free-to-Play Rule

Metaworld is free to download, enter, and play.

Free-to-play does **not** mean the in-world economy has no value. Players begin with the established minimal survival start and must build their life through play, work, trade, professions, business, exploration, cooperation, crime, combat, crafting, property, and other systems.

Marketplace goods, property, services, player-created media, business admissions, vehicle use, creator products, and other economic activity can carry real in-world value.

Core player access to the game must never require purchasing combat power with real money.

---

# 1. Marketplace Meshes Are Never Zero-Value Products

Creator-enabled Metaworld meshes are platform-controlled assets and are not treated as free marketplace inventory.

The player avatar body, world geometry, required gameplay infrastructure, and other non-marketplace technical assets are not treated as store products merely because they are meshes.

For **sellable creator-enabled objects**, the economic model is:

Metaworld Base Asset Value
+
Creator Texture/Product Value
=
Final Player Product Price

The creator does not receive ownership of the underlying Metaworld mesh. The creator receives the right to publish approved texture products against compatible base assets under the creator system.

A sale can therefore contain multiple ledger components:

- Base Asset ID
- Base Asset Value
- Creator Product ID
- Creator Price / Markup
- Gross Sale Price
- Currency
- Metaworld marketplace commission rate
- Metaworld commission amount
- Creator net proceeds
- Buyer entitlement
- Seller/creator ID
- Timestamp

The exact percentage and pricing formulas remain server-configurable.

Metaworld may earn from both the underlying platform asset value and a small marketplace commission, but pricing should remain attractive enough that creators can build profitable businesses.

---

# 2. Creator Sales Must Benefit Both Creator and Metaworld

Every normal creator marketplace sale generates a small Metaworld/platform share.

Core rule:

> Metaworld grows when its creators grow.

The creator should receive the majority of creator proceeds. Metaworld receives a configurable platform percentage for operating the asset ecosystem, marketplace, transaction ledger, discovery, hosting, moderation, entitlement system, security, and game infrastructure.

Commission policy should be centrally configurable so different categories can be balanced later without rewriting product data.

Possible categories include:

- Texture products
- Clothing appearances
- Furniture appearances
- Vehicle liveries
- Weapon skins
- Building surface products
- Music sales
- Video/film sales where supported
- Business admissions/tickets
- Venue rentals
- Property transactions
- Player services

Not every category must use the same percentage.

---

# 3. Player Music Economy

Players can upload and sell music they have the legal right to distribute.

Supported creator roles can include:

- Musician
- Singer
- Producer
- DJ
- Composer
- Label/collective owner
- Venue operator

Possible music products/services:

- Song purchase
- Album/collection purchase
- In-world jukebox access
- Club playlist licensing
- DJ event tickets
- Concert tickets
- Radio programming
- Business background-music licensing if later supported

Every uploaded track should have:

- Media Asset ID
- Creator/uploader ID
- Rights declaration
- Title/artist metadata
- File validation status
- Moderation status
- Sale price
- Currency
- Usage permissions
- Venue/playback permissions
- Transaction history

Players may only sell or commercially broadcast music they own or are authorized to distribute. Metaworld should support reporting/takedown procedures and retain upload/rights/audit records.

Room-, property-, club-, and vehicle-scoped playback continues to use the Media Session architecture defined elsewhere.

---

# 4. Player Video, Cinema & Drive-In Businesses

Players can run entertainment businesses such as:

- Drive-in theaters
- Cinemas
- Private screening rooms
- Clubs with video screens
- Event halls
- Player-made film festivals
- News/documentary screenings

A venue owner can schedule a screening and charge admission.

Example transaction:

Player buys ticket
-> Ticket entitlement issued
-> Venue access granted for event window
-> Metaworld commission recorded
-> Venue/operator proceeds recorded
-> Media playback available only to authorized attendees in the correct media zone

The video/film must be content the uploader or operator is authorized to distribute or exhibit commercially.

Metaworld should not assume that arbitrary movies from outside the game can be uploaded and resold.

Player-made movies, machinima, documentaries, music videos, advertisements, training media, event recordings, and other authorized productions can become a real creator industry inside Metaworld.

---

# 5. Metaworld News Is Different From Player Entertainment Media

Official Metaworld News is generated from verified world records and the Event Ledger.

Player-created entertainment media is creator content.

These systems may interact but must remain distinguishable:

- Official verified news
- Player journalism
- Player entertainment
- Advertising
- Fiction/film
- Rumor/opinion if later supported

A player can run a cinema showing a documentary about a famous zombie attack, while the official Metaworld News archive retains the verified historical record of that event.

---

# 6. No Magical Safe Zones

Metaworld has no location that becomes immune to the living world simply because it is a social, commercial, residential, religious, medical, government, entertainment, or beginner-oriented space.

Potentially affected places include:

- Houses
- Apartments
- Clubs
- Bars
- Drive-ins
- Cinemas
- Stores
- Hospitals
- Police stations
- Churches/temples
- Schools/training locations
- Markets
- Farms
- Wilderness camps
- Player businesses
- Town centers
- Roads
- Parking lots

A zombie outbreak, crime, faction attack, fire, storm, blackout, supernatural event, riot, robbery, siege, or other valid world event can affect any location when world conditions allow it.

Core rule:

> Safety is produced by people, systems, preparation, infrastructure, law, and defense — not by invisible invulnerability walls.

---

# 7. Security Is a Profession and an Economy

Because there are no absolute safe zones, peace itself creates gameplay roles and businesses.

Players/NPCs can become:

- Police officers
- Guards
- Private security
- Bouncers
- Bodyguards
- Neighborhood watch
- Convoy escorts
- Event security
- Property security managers
- Emergency responders
- Military/faction defenders where appropriate

Property and businesses can invest in:

- Locks
- Gates
- Walls
- Cameras
- Alarms
- Lighting
- Secure rooms
- Guard NPCs
- Player guards
- Defensive construction
- Emergency exits
- Backup power
- Medical supplies
- Fire suppression
- Vehicle barriers
- Communication systems

Higher security makes a place harder or riskier to attack, but does not make it magically untouchable.

---

# 8. Law and Consequences Replace Artificial Safety

The absence of safe zones does not mean attacks are consequence-free.

Aggressive actions can generate:

- Witness events
- Camera/security records
- Crime records
- Wanted status
- Reputation changes
- Player/NPC police response
- Arrest
- Court cases
- Fines
- Jail gameplay
- Faction retaliation
- Business blacklisting
- Bounties where allowed
- Property damage claims
- Insurance consequences if that system is added
- News coverage
- Historical records for major incidents

Real platform moderation remains separate from in-world law enforcement.

Gameplay crime is part of Metaworld. Harassment, cheating, exploits, or platform-rule violations are handled by actual moderation systems.

---

# 9. Persistent Ownership — Logging Out Does Not Pause the World

Metaworld is persistent. Logging out does **not** freeze, hide, phase out, or automatically protect a player's land, home, business, vehicles, storage, crops, hired NPCs, or other owned assets.

Core rule:

> If you own something valuable, you are responsible for arranging its protection before you leave it unattended.

Protection can come from systems such as:

- Hired NPC guards
- Trusted player guards
- Police friends/relationships
- Local police protection where law and response capacity permit
- Player security organizations
- Faction/guild/community protection
- Neighbors
- Walls, fences, gates and defensive construction
- Locks and access controls
- Cameras and evidence systems
- Alarms and remote alerts
- Guard animals/creatures if later supported
- Automated defenses where the game permits them
- Hidden/secure storage
- Vehicle locks/garages
- Backup power
- Emergency plans
- Strong reputation or political relationships

None of these are magical invulnerability. They are actual systems that can succeed or fail.

## Offline Raid and Theft

While the owner is offline, hostile players or hostile/evil NPCs may be able to:

- Trespass
- Break or bypass defenses if capable
- Steal loose items
- Break into storage
- Steal vehicles
- Damage structures
- Damage utilities
- Kill or defeat guards/NPC residents
- Occupy buildings
- Loot businesses
- Sabotage property
- Claim or seize property when conquest/claim conditions are satisfied

The same world rules apply whether the defender is online or offline. Logging out is not a defensive ability.

## Property Seizure / Claim

Taking land should be a world action, not a one-click ownership exploit.

A valid seizure system can require conditions such as:

- Physical access to the property
- Defeating/bypassing active defenses
- Controlling a deed/claim point/property authority object
- Holding/occupying the property for a configured claim period
- Meeting regional law/faction/conquest rules
- Paying any required claim, repair, debt, tax, or registration costs where applicable
- Surviving counterattack during the claim process

Different regions may have different legal consequences, but the world must permit genuine loss of ownership when the rules are satisfied.

Hostile NPC factions or intelligent evil NPCs can use the same claim framework where appropriate. An NPC gang, raider group, corrupt faction, or other hostile force can take over a poorly defended property rather than merely despawning when the player logs in.

## What the Returning Owner Sees

When the player logs back in, Metaworld loads the world **as it actually became**.

They may discover:

- Missing inventory
- A stolen vehicle
- Dead/injured guards
- Broken doors or walls
- Damaged utilities
- An occupied home
- A hostile flag/faction presence
- A changed deed/claim state
- A business that was looted
- A property that has been fully taken

There is no automatic rollback merely because the owner was offline.

The returning player can respond through gameplay:

- Call police
- Use evidence/camera records
- Pursue thieves
- File an in-world legal claim where applicable
- Hire fighters/security
- Recruit allies
- Negotiate
- Pay ransom
- Retake the property by force where allowed
- Hunt the hostile NPC/player group
- Rebuild somewhere else

This turns property ownership into responsibility, politics, alliances, security, law, warfare, business, and reputation rather than a static inventory entitlement.

## Event Ledger and News

Major raids, seizures, murders, famous defenses, criminal takeovers, faction occupations, and property recoveries should be recorded by the Event Ledger when they meet significance/visibility rules.

A player may literally log back in and learn from Metaworld News that their district was attacked while they were gone.

# 10. Example: Drive-In Night

A player owns land outside town and builds a drive-in theater.

They purchase or produce approved screen/equipment assets.

They license or upload a film they are authorized to exhibit.

They schedule a Friday-night screening.

Other players buy tickets.

Cars arrive and park.

The venue owner sells food and drinks.

A DJ or musician sells music before the movie.

Security players are hired for the event.

The movie starts through a property-scoped Media Session.

Midway through the screening, a zombie event reaches the area.

The film does not trigger a safe-zone shield.

Security reacts.

Players use their cars, weapons, improvised objects, magic, medical skills, and teamwork.

The venue owner may choose to defend the business, evacuate customers, or abandon it.

Police/emergency players and NPCs may respond depending on region and availability.

The Event Ledger records the attack, property damage, deaths, rescues, arrests, and heroic actions.

Later, Metaworld News reports the event.

The drive-in can reopen, rebuild, upgrade security, raise ticket prices, hire better guards, or become famous because of what happened there.

This is the target: business, media, economy, danger, professions, vehicles, combat, social life, and world history all interacting naturally.

---

# 11. Core Principle

Metaworld is free to play, but it is not an empty free economy.

Players create value.

Players sell value.

Players own value.

Players must protect value.

Players and hostile NPCs can threaten, steal, occupy, damage, or seize value under world rules and accept the resulting consequences.

Metaworld provides the platform and receives a small share of supported economic activity.

No place is automatically safe, and logging out is not protection.

Peace exists because somebody built it, paid for it, defended it, enforced it, negotiated it, allied for it, or earned it.
